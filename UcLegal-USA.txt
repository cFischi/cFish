Notes

- Scope: This list includes the Federalist Papers, core U.S. founding documents, influential founding-era texts (1770s–1790s), and key documents cited in Supreme Court rulings (e.g., Marbury v. Madison (1803), McCulloch v. Maryland (1819), District of Columbia v. Heller (2008)). It is comprehensive but not exhaustive due to the vast number of citations over two centuries.
- Usage: Documents are referenced for framers’ intent, historical context, or legal principles (e.g., Bill of Rights as binding law, Magna Carta for due process). Founding-era texts like the Virginia Plan shaped constitutional debates, while philosophical works (e.g., Locke) informed principles.
- Sources: URLs are from stable, public-domain repositories (National Archives, Library of Congress, Yale’s Avalon Project, Liberty Fund). Some documents (e.g., state ratification debates) are partially digitized; representative sources are provided.
- Verification: All URLs verified active and accessible as of April 21, 2025. Prompt Grok for updates if links fail.
- Limitations: Obscure documents (e.g., minor colonial statutes, full state convention records) lack centralized URLs and are omitted for practicality. Request specific documents or case citations for further additions.
